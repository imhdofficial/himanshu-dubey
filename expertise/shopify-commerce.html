THE STANDARD

Commerce design is decision design.

Every page should reduce a buying question: what is this, is it right for me, can I trust it, what happens next, and how easily can I complete the purchase?

DISCOVERYCollections + merchandising
DECISIONProduct information hierarchy
COMMITCart confidence
SYSTEMReusable theme sections
WHAT GREAT LOOKS LIKE
01

Merchandise with hierarchy

Collection and product pages should prioritize the attributes that actually influence purchase, not treat every piece of content equally.

02

Make the theme reusable

Build sections and blocks that give the store flexibility without letting every page become visually inconsistent.

03

Mobile is the primary purchase surface

Navigation, product variants, sticky actions, cart behavior and campaign banners must be designed for the thumb first.

04

Protect purchase context

Selections, cart state and user progress should survive predictable navigation and interaction changes.

05

Keep Liquid readable

Separate data, presentation and reusable snippets so future edits do not require risky copy-paste changes.

06

Speed supports conversion

Heavy media and script bloat directly damage the perception of quality and the ease of buying.

PRACTICAL METHOD
01

Map buying intents

Separate everyday browsing, gifting, campaigns and high-intent product visits.

02

Design reusable merchandising

Create section patterns that can be rearranged without losing hierarchy.

03

Engineer theme components

Use Liquid snippets, sections, schema and predictable CSS/JS ownership.

04

Test purchase paths

Verify variants, cart, discounts, out-of-stock states and mobile journeys.

05

Measure friction

Use analytics and observed drop-offs to refine discovery and checkout-adjacent experiences.
